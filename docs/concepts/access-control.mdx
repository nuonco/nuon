Every identity in your Nuon org — whether a team member or a [service account](/concepts/service-accounts) — must be assigned a **role**. The role determines what that identity can read and change in the org.

Roles are org-scoped: a role assigned in one org grants no access in any other org.

## Built-in roles

Every org comes with two assignable built-in roles. They cover the whole org and cannot be edited or deleted.

| Role | Value | Grants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Admin** | `org_admin` | Full access to the org and everything in it, including team and credential management. |
| **Read-only** | `org_read_only` | Read-only access to everything in the org; cannot make changes. |

## Custom roles

When neither built-in role fits, an org admin can create a **custom role** whose write access is confined to
specific resources — one app, a set of installs, an app's branches. A custom role is a list of permission
entries, each naming what it applies to and which of create, read, update, and delete it grants.

Custom roles are assigned through exactly the same flows as built-in ones, and an identity still holds one role at
a time. See [Custom roles](/concepts/custom-roles) for the permission model and
[Creating custom roles](/guides/custom-roles) for worked examples.

<Warning>
**Reads are org-wide; writes are what you scope.** Listing endpoints — every "show me my installs" or "show me my
apps" view — are authorized at the org level, and they are not filtered to the resources a role names. A role that
can read anything can read everything in the org. Scope a custom role to restrict what it can *change*, not what it
can see.
</Warning>

Because of that, a custom role almost always needs org-level `read` alongside its scoped write entries. Without it
the role can open a resource it owns by direct link but every list comes back empty, which looks like a broken role
rather than a missing permission. The dashboard's **Read everything, write to specific installs** preset builds this
combination for you.

## Assigning roles

The same role set applies everywhere an identity is created. You choose a role when you:

- [Invite a team member](/guides/team-management) — or change an existing member's role
- Create a [service account](/concepts/service-accounts)
- Create an [API token](/concepts/api-tokens)
- Create an [OIDC trust policy](/concepts/oidc-federation)

[API tokens](/concepts/api-tokens) and [OIDC trust policies](/concepts/oidc-federation) are each backed by a dedicated [service account](/concepts/service-accounts) that carries the role — so a token or an exchanged token acts as that service account, with exactly the access its role grants.

<Note>
An identity holds one role at a time; assigning a new role replaces the old one.
</Note>

Built-in roles are addressed by their value (`org_admin`, `org_read_only`); custom roles are addressed by their
role id, since every custom role shares the same underlying type. Anywhere you pass `--role`, either form works.
Run `nuon roles list` to see what each role expects.

## Permission errors

When an identity attempts something its role does not allow, Nuon returns a message describing the access the action requires and the role you currently hold — for example, "this action requires write access to installs in this organization." Ask an org admin to assign a role that grants the needed access.

## Reserved roles

Some built-in roles exist for Nuon's own machine accounts and internal support flows. They are assigned
automatically, never offered in any picker, and cannot be selected: **Runner** (the accounts Nuon provisions for
your runners), **Installer**, and **Support**.
